The activities

🌊 Catch the Wave + Wave Lab

Wave parts, a water dot that shows waves move energy (not water), then a fair test: collect data, plot the graph, and write a coached CER.

4.02 Amplitude and energy · 45–75 minTry it →

🎯 Buoy Training

Read real buoy water-level graphs. Still water is 0, so wave height = crest − trough and change = end − start. Coaching for every sign mistake.

4.01 + integer warm-ups · 10–15 minTry it →

⚡ Wave Power Engineer

Pick one of 12 NOAA buoys, design a machine on a budget, survive a 48-hour storm, earn stars, then write “Could we really power Ohio?”

4.09 Review or Science Friday · 45–60 minTry it →

🔊 Sound Relay

Seven design challenges with everyday things: a whisper across the room, a knock through a wall, a spoon on a string, underwater, a speed race, outer space, and soundproofing. Ends with “Can astronauts hear in space?”

4.05 · 4.06 · 4.07 · 30–45 minTry it →

🕵️ Quake Detective

Watch longitudinal P-waves and transverse S-waves, read seismograms, find the S − P gap, and use 3 stations to locate the real 2019 Lake Erie earthquake. Ends with “Why does the P-wave arrive first?”

4.11 · 4.12 · 45–60 minTry it →

How a lesson works

Post the student link

Students tap the activity you name. No login, nothing to install.

Students explore

Every step has 🔊 Read to me, and a toolbar for Tap to hear, bigger text, spacing, contrast and fewer choices.

Test and improve

Predict, test, read the graph or bar chart, and change one thing. Stars reward good engineering, not speed.

Coached CER

A coach checks the claim, evidence and reasoning one part at a time, with an example on a different topic and sentence frames.

Turn it in

📋 Copy for Canvas pastes into Text Entry with the data table. Students can also save a PDF or a text file.

What students learn

ActivityScience and mathOhio standard
Catch the WaveWaves transfer energy, not water. Taller waves (bigger amplitude) carry more energy. Wave energy is renewable.7.PS.4
Wave Lab + CERFair test with one variable; graphing; when wave height doubles, power is about 4 times bigger; energy is never created, only caught.7.PS.4, science inquiry
Buoy TrainingReading line graphs; positive and negative values around 0; subtracting a negative is adding.Math 7.NS.1
Wave Power Engineer + Power Ohio CEREngineering trade-offs with real NOAA wave heights; feasibility; Lake Erie waves are too small to power Ohio.7.PS.4, real wave data
Quake Detective + P-wave CERP-waves are longitudinal and S-waves are transverse; both are mechanical waves through rock; P-waves are faster; reading real seismic data; S − P gap → distance; locating an epicenter.7.PS.4, real wave data, Math 7.NS
Sound Relay + Space CERSound is a longitudinal, mechanical wave that needs a medium; solids and liquids carry it well; soft materials absorb it; light and radio are electromagnetic.7.PS.4

Standards guardrail: in the wave energy activities, the time between waves stays at 8 seconds, so power depends only on wave height. Students never measure wave period against energy, which keeps the data consistent with Ohio’s amplitude-and-energy statement. Details are in the Teacher Guide.

Made for classrooms

Everything runs in the browser with nothing to install. There are no accounts, ads or chat, and no personal information is sent anywhere. Work and settings are saved only in the browser on that device, so the Canvas copy is each student’s real record. When Wave Power Engineer checks for new buoy readings, the browser asks NOAA’s public data servers for wave numbers; no student information goes with that request.
