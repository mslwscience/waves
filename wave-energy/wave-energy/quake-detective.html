Quake Detective

🕵️ 0 of 4 missions

An earthquake shook Ohio! Can you find where it started?

P-wave (primary): a longitudinal wave. Rock particles push and pull along the direction the wave moves. Faster. Arrives first.
S-wave (secondary): a transverse wave. Rock particles move up and down, across the direction the wave moves. Slower. Arrives second.

Both are mechanical waves: they carry energy through the rock of the Earth.

Mission 1: Watch P-waves and S-waves

Press Send the earthquake. Watch how the red particle moves in each wave, and which wave reaches the seismograph first.

Mission 2: Read a seismogram

A seismogram is a graph of how much the ground shakes over time.

1. P-wave = where the line first starts to wiggle. 2. S-wave = where the wiggles suddenly get much bigger.

3. Gap = S time − P time. 4. Distance = gap × 8 km. (Every second of gap means about 8 km away.)

Mission 3: Find the real earthquake

Three seismograph stations felt the same earthquake. Read each one to find how far away the quake was. Each distance draws a circle. The quake is where all three circles cross.

Seismograms are modeled from the real earthquake’s location, using typical speeds in Earth’s crust: P-waves about 6 km/s, S-waves about 3.4 km/s. Map is simplified.
